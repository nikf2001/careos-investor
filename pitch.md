# CareOS — One-Page Pitch

**Pre-Seed | March 2026 | 3–5M NOK**

---

## 1. The Problem

Norway's 80+ population will more than double by 2040. The healthcare workforce cannot grow proportionally — NOU 2023:4 projects a 28,000-worker deficit by 2035. Municipal nursing homes already spend 8–18M NOK per facility annually on temporary agency staff to cover chronic shortages.

The tools exist to help: VR training platforms (Making View), competency management systems (Dossier), scheduling software (GAT, Visma), and soon robotic logistics. But these systems are disconnected silos. Competency data doesn't inform scheduling. Training completion doesn't update shift eligibility. Staffing changes don't trigger risk recalculations.

The result: reactive operations, preventable adverse events, and unsustainable costs. Care workers waste 30–40% of their time on logistics and administration instead of patient care. Falls, medication errors, and unnecessary hospitalizations cost 1–3M NOK per facility annually — most of them preventable with better information flow.

No one owns the layer that connects these systems. That's the opportunity.

---

## 2. The Solution

CareOS is the healthcare operations OS — a horizontal data platform, workflow engine, and AI decision support layer with pluggable connectors for every tool a care facility uses.

**4-layer architecture:**

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| **Data & Identity** | Unified staff profiles aggregating competency, training, scheduling, and certification data from all connected systems |
| **Workflow & Automation** | Event-driven engine that orchestrates cross-system processes: competency gap → training assignment → readiness update → shift eligibility recalculation |
| **Intelligence & Decision Support** | AI-powered risk scoring, staffing optimization, and predictive alerts based on aggregated operational data |
| **Integration Framework** | Pluggable connector architecture with standardized adapter pattern, HL7 FHIR native support, and a connector SDK for third-party vendors |

CareOS does not compete with any tool vendor. We make every connected tool more valuable. VR vendors, scheduling providers, and robotics companies are ecosystem partners — not competitors. This is the iOS model applied to healthcare operations: we build the platform, others build the apps.

**First vertical wedge:** Dossier (competency/HR) + Making View (VR training) connectors, proving the platform through the competency-to-readiness workflow.

**TAM: 4–6B NOK** — Total operations technology spend for Norwegian municipal care facilities (~1,000 nursing homes, 10,000+ home care units).

**SAM: 500M–1B NOK** — Medium-to-large nursing homes (50+ beds) in municipalities with budget and mandate for interoperability. ~400–600 facilities.

**SOM: 50–100M NOK** — 30–60 facilities within 3–5 years at 1–2M NOK annual revenue per facility.

**Regulatory tailwinds:** NOU 2023:4 mandates technology-driven workforce transformation. HL7 FHIR adoption requirements favour interoperable platforms. Velferdsteknologiprogrammet provides earmarked municipal funding for health technology. The political and regulatory environment has never been more favourable for CareOS.

**Demographics are non-negotiable.** Unlike market trends that can reverse, the aging population is a locked-in structural force. Every year, the problem gets worse — and the value of the solution increases.

## 4. Competitive Advantage

**No direct competitor exists.** The healthcare operations OS category in Norwegian municipal care is unoccupied. Adjacent players are potential partners, not threats:

| Player | Role in CareOS Ecosystem |
|---|---|
| Dossier | Connector partner (competency data source) |
| Making View | Connector partner (training event source) |
| Visma / GAT | Future connector partner (scheduling data) |
| Temp agencies | Disruption target (CareOS reduces dependency) |

**Three structural moats:**

1. **Data gravity.** Once operational data flows through CareOS, the accumulated cross-system intelligence creates switching costs that grow over time. A competitor would need to replicate the full data model and all connector mappings simultaneously.

2. **Vendor-neutral positioning.** The OS layer is structurally non-threatening to tool vendors. Every vendor has incentive to build a CareOS connector because it makes their product more valuable — creating a distribution flywheel that a vertically integrated competitor cannot replicate.

3. **Workflow intelligence.** Cross-system automation (competency gap → training → readiness → scheduling) is impossible with point solutions. The more systems connected, the more valuable the orchestration layer becomes. This is a compounding advantage.

**Market timing is optimal.** Five years ago, not enough tools existed to make an OS layer valuable. Five years from now, incumbents will have built proprietary integrations. The window is now.

## 5. Business Model

**Platform + Connector SaaS pricing:**

- **Core platform license:** 300K–1.5M NOK/year per facility (tiered by size and feature set)
- **Per-connector fees:** 50K–200K NOK/year per connector (Dossier, Making View, scheduling, robotics, EPJ)
- **Average revenue per facility (ARPF):** 600K–1.2M NOK/year

**Built for municipal procurement:** Pricing fits within existing IT budget line items. At 500K–1M NOK, CareOS costs 3–6% of annual temp agency spend — positioning it as a cost reduction tool with 2–4x Year 1 ROI.

**Net revenue retention target: 120%+** driven by connector expansion — facilities start with 1–2 connectors and add more as they see ROI.

**Gross margin: 75–85%** at scale (pure SaaS, minimal per-facility marginal cost).
